Retail Demand Forecasting

An end-to-end SKU pipeline that enforces chronological evaluation, compares a transparent Ridge model with a seasonal baseline, quantifies uncertainty, and converts forecasts into reorder decisions.

The problem

A useful forecast must beat a baseline and change a decision.

Retail teams do not benefit from an accuracy number disconnected from replenishment. This project evaluates each SKU on the most recent period, reports bias and interval coverage alongside point error, and translates residual uncertainty into safety stock and reorder points.

Evaluation principle

Performance is never presented without the seven-day seasonal-naive benchmark. The pipeline measures whether the model adds value beyond repeating last week's demand.

Data and modeling

Every feature is shifted before it can see the target.

  1. Prepare demand history

    Generate reproducible multi-SKU data or ingest UCI Online Retail II, exclude returns, and aggregate transactions to daily unit demand.

  2. Create leakage-safe predictors

    Build lags, shifted rolling statistics, calendar attributes, price, and promotion signals only from information available at prediction time.

  3. Hold out the future

    Train on earlier observations and reserve the latest horizon for chronological backtesting rather than random splitting.

  4. Compare and quantify

    Evaluate Ridge and seasonal naive with WAPE, MAE, RMSE, bias, improvement, and empirical 90% interval coverage.

  5. Plan replenishment

    Convert residual variability and a service-level assumption into per-SKU safety stock and reorder points.

Engineering choices

Transparency is more valuable than unnecessary model complexity.

Ridge regression is stable under correlated lag features and easy to audit. The command-line pipeline produces the same artifacts for synthetic and real-data paths, while automated tests check nonnegative forecasts, ordered intervals, metric identities, baseline improvement, feature completeness, and positive inventory policies.

Deliverables

The pipeline leaves an auditable evidence trail.

  • report.html for a human-readable portfolio report.
  • backtest_forecasts.csv for actual, forecast, baseline, and interval values.
  • sku_metrics_inventory.csv for error metrics and replenishment recommendations.
  • portfolio_summary.json for machine-readable rollups.

Limits & next steps

The demonstration proves the workflow, not a production business outcome.

  • The default data is synthetic; UCI support is an adapter, not evidence of a deployed retailer forecast.
  • Intermittent demand may require Croston-style methods rather than Ridge.
  • Production work should model stockouts and lead-time uncertainty and use rolling-origin evaluation.
  • Multi-level retail planning would benefit from hierarchical reconciliation across SKU, category, and location.
Next milestone

Add rolling-origin model selection and inventory-cost simulation so forecast improvement is evaluated in service-level and holding-cost terms.
