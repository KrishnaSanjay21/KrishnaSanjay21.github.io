US Homicide Clearance Analysis

A 1976-2023 analysis of FBI Supplementary Homicide Report records examining how the growing share of stranger and unknown-offender cases relates to the long-run decline in national clearance rates.

The question

Why have clearance rates declined as the composition of homicide changed?

The analysis organizes nearly five decades of case data around a specific hypothesis: cases involving strangers or unknown offenders provide fewer relationship-based investigative leads and have become a larger share of homicide. Those categories are consolidated into an interpretable annual anonymity measure.

Interpretation boundary

The reported correlation is an association, not proof that anonymity alone caused the decline. Reporting changes, omitted factors, policing resources, geography, and time trends can also influence both measures.

Analysis pipeline

Feature engineering connects case composition to national trends.

  1. Prepare the SHR record

    Clean case-level fields from 1976-2023 and aggregate outcomes to annual national measures.

  2. Define anonymous cases

    Combine stranger relationships and unknown-offender records into one documented feature.

  3. Explore associations

    Compare clearance and anonymity trends and quantify the reported negative correlation.

  4. Test model behavior

    Compare polynomial regression, random forest, and support-vector regression for historical fit and extrapolation behavior.

  5. Communicate evidence

    Package the analysis in notebook, Plotly and Power BI visuals, and a formal LaTeX PDF report.

Model decision

The best historical fit was not the best forecasting choice.

The repository reports that random forest fit the observed period strongly but flattened when asked to extrapolate beyond the training range, a structural limitation of tree ensembles. A degree-three polynomial was selected because it could express the nonlinear long-run trajectory, with reported R-squared of 0.81 and MAE of 1.32%.

That choice improves extrapolation mechanics; it does not make a long-range forecast causal or certain. A time-index polynomial can absorb multiple unmeasured social and institutional shifts.

Deliverables

The work is designed for both technical review and policy communication.

  • Exploratory and modeling notebook with the transformation pipeline.
  • Correlation matrices and forecast visualizations in Plotly and Power BI.
  • Formal PDF report documenting methodology, findings, and recommendations.
  • Model comparison that explicitly discusses the extrapolation failure of tree-based methods.

Limits & next steps

Stronger inference requires more structure than a national time trend.

  • Validate missingness, reporting coverage, and definition changes across years and agencies.
  • Model state or agency panels with fixed effects rather than only national aggregates.
  • Use time-aware validation, structural-break tests, and uncertainty bands for forecasts.
  • Separate unsolved known-offender cases from truly unknown-offender cases where the data supports it.
Next milestone

Build a state-year panel with covariates and robustness checks, then report how sensitive the anonymity association is to geography, reporting coverage, and alternative definitions.
